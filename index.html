Compact Oak Home

A small footprint that needs to feel useful, calm and visually open at the same time.

Goal. Add storage and clearer movement without making the home feel tighter.

Core move. Treat joinery, circulation and warm timber as one system.

Direction. Let vertical space work harder while daylight keeps the room light.

02 · BathroomConcept study

Brass Bath House

A compact bathroom shaped around quiet function and one controlled material language.

Goal. Give a small room character without over-decorating it.

Core move. Keep the shell simple and let timber, brass and proportion carry the identity.

Direction. Detail where touch matters; simplify everywhere else.

03 · Living roomConcept study

Liberty Hearth

A gathering room where architecture stays in charge and furniture supports it.

Goal. Build warmth and presence without crowding the fireplace or ceiling structure.

Core move. Use the hearth as the anchor and keep the furniture rhythm low.

Direction. Strong focal point, restrained contrast, generous breathing room.

04 · Living roomConcept study

Accord Living

A generous room balancing heavy timber, big openings and everyday comfort.

Goal. Keep the architecture warm without letting it become visually heavy.

Core move. Give beams breathing room and keep upholstery quiet.

Direction. Natural structure first; comfort layered around it.

Process

Know what happens next.

Five stages. Each one closes a different uncertainty before the next one begins.

  1. 01

    Consult

    Define the problem: space, goals, budget and constraints.

  2. 02

    Plan

    Resolve the structure: flow, zoning, storage and priorities.

  3. 03

    Design

    Build the language: materials, light, furniture and details.

  4. 04

    Approve + coordinate

    Lock critical choices before build decisions move.

  5. 05

    Handover + support

    Close the loop: final checks, documentation and agreed follow-up.
